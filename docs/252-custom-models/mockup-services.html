Settings → Services

The Settings → Services screen. You start empty, press Add service, and pick service → billing mode → credential. There is no “Available” block: the catalogue lives in the dialog. One card per service × billing mode, matching what the model picker groups on — and a subscription card is where the accounts, their order, and the routing settings live. Interactive: add, reorder, remove.

Services

One card per service and billing mode. A subscription card holds every account you have with that service and decides how ShipIt picks between them.

req 12Routing settings live on the subscription card, because that is the thing they route within. Order, Use in order vs Spread across accounts, and the failover cutoffs are all answers to “which of these accounts next?” — a question that only exists where there is a group to choose from.
Today these are per-provider settings (selectionMode, isPrimary, failoverCutoffs in ProviderAccountsCard). Per service × mode is the same setting re-keyed, not a new one.
req 12An API-key card has no routing controls at all — not a disabled group, not an empty section, and no sentence explaining the absence. Keys do not fail over, so there is nothing to order and nothing to spread. The asymmetry between the two card types is req 12, rendered.
A draft carried “No failover: an API key is metered billing…” on every key card. Cut: it repeats verbatim once per key service, and the missing controls already say it — the same reason the harness menu does not announce that its set is fixed.
req 5The card is the picker’s group. An earlier draft of this variant made every credential its own row, which meant two Anthropic subscriptions were two rows in Settings and one group in the picker — the two surfaces counting differently. Grouping by service × mode removes that mismatch instead of explaining it, and gives the routing settings somewhere to live. Those turned out to be the same fix.
req 7The catalogue moves into the dialog. Variant A shows every service ShipIt knows in an “Available” block, so the screen grows with the catalogue whether or not you use any of it. Here the steady-state screen is only what you configured.
Discoverability was weighed and accepted: someone who needs OpenRouter or Vercel is looking for it, and will find it in the dialog.
req 5Billing mode is picked at add time. Step 2 asks how do you pay for it and shows “Subscription — 1 model” against “API key — 2 models”, so the distinction the picker groups on is one the user makes deliberately rather than discovers later.
