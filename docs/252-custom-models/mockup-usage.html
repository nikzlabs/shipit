Usage, split by where the money went

Prototype for docs/252-custom-models. Today the usage modal shows one Cost figure, because there is one provider and one way of paying. Once a session can move between a subscription and a metered key — even within a single service — one total silently adds money you spent to tokens that were already paid for. This splits it on the same axis everything else in this feature splits on: service × billing mode.

req 11Two headline numbers, because there are two kinds of thing. Dollars and quota do not add up, so they are not added up. “You paid” sums the metered rows only — the one figure that is money — and everything on a plan is counted in turns beside it.
A single Cost stat is the current design and it is fine today, when a session has one provider and one way of paying. It stops being fine the moment a session can move between a subscription and a key.
req 5The split is service × billing mode — the same grouping as the picker and Settings. The two DeepSeek rows are the proof it has to be the mode and not the service: one service, two lines, and merging them would put “$0.11” against a row that is mostly free.
req 10Quota belongs to the subscription rows only. A metered row shows a price and no bar; a plan row shows a bar and no price. That is req 10’s “no indicator at all” rule and req 12’s billing-mode branch showing up in a third place.
openWhat is costUsd when the turn was on a subscription? ShipIt records the CLI’s figure uniformly today (usage.ts), with no notion of billing mode — so the same stored number may be money spent or a notional API-equivalent, depending on how the turn was authenticated. Which one each harness reports under subscription auth has to be established before this screen can be trusted; the mock assumes the honest reading and shows no dollar figure on plan rows.

req 11Plan usage carries its API-rate value. “≈ $243.60 at API rates” is the number that says whether a subscription is worth keeping, and hiding it to protect the paid/included distinction was overcautious — the distinction is carried by colour, wording and position, not by the absence of a second figure.
