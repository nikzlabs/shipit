The convention this is up against. CLAUDE.md and the design-language skill both say icons are always Phosphor: the hand-written <svg> exception is for a bespoke illustration in an empty or onboarding state, and it says plainly that “anything that labels an action, a status, or a row is an icon, and icons are always Phosphor, no exceptions”. A Role glyph labels a row and a chip, so adopting one of these means amending that rule — not finding a loophole in it.

What drawing them actually settled. Five of the six are disqualified by what they sit next to, not by their idea: an arrow under an arrow, a triangle where the app already uses triangles for warnings, a hamburger, a “blocked” sign. That is a hazard a custom set carries and a stock set does not — Phosphor's shapes were drawn to be distinct from each other, and a mark drawn on its own has to be checked against every neighbour by hand, forever, as those neighbours change.

Brace is the one that survives, and it is genuinely good on meaning: three things gathered under one name is the mechanism of this feature, which is the exception's own test — “depicts a relationship a glyph set cannot”. Whether it beats BriefcaseIcon is a taste call, and it is yours. What it costs is the rule: adopting it means amending the design language, not slipping past it.
